The Binomial Distribution: 13C Atoms in Cholesterol

Exercise 1. The figure on the right is a barplot that shows, for a sample of 100 molecules of cholesterol, the number that have 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, or 5 atoms of 13C, as determined by mass spectrometry. Is this data continuous or discrete? How do you know?

Exercise 2. The data in this experiment follows a binomial distribution in which the probability of a particular outcome, \(p(x)\), is given by the equation \[p(x) = \frac{n!}{x!(n - x)!} \times p^{x} \times (1 - p)^{n - x}\] where n is the number of carbons atoms in a molecule of cholesterol (also called the size), x is the number of 13C atoms in a molecule of cholesterol, and p is the probability that a randomly selected carbon atom is 13C. The exclamation point indicates a factorial (e.g. \(3! = 3 \times 2 \times 1 = 6\)). Do some research to determine (i) the number of carbon atoms in cholesterol and (ii) the probability that an atom of carbon is 13C. Adjust the sliders to these values and comment on the fit of the equation to the data.

Exercise 3. The mean, \(\mu\), for a binomial distribution is \(n \times p\) and its standard deviation, \(\sigma\) is \(\sqrt{n \times p \times (1-p)}\). Report the mean and the standard deviation for this data using your values for \(n\) and \(p\), and then download the data as a spreadsheet and check your result by calculating the actual mean and standard deviation.
